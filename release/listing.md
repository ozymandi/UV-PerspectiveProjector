## Name

Perspective Projector

## Tagline (from the manifest)

Match a camera to a photo and project it onto geometry

## Description

Turn a single photo into a camera-matched 3D set in three clicks, without leaving Blender.

**1. Calibrate**

- Load a photo and press Calibrate.
- The add-on finds the vanishing points and works out the focal length and camera rotation.
- It creates a projection camera with the photo as its background and sets the render resolution to the photo size.
- If the photo is hard to read, the line editor opens with the best guess already placed. This happens with one-point views, organic scenes and cropped photos. Drag the handles onto real edges and the camera updates live.

**2. Project**

- With nothing selected, Project creates a card that exactly fills the camera's frame. With meshes selected (your own blockout), it projects onto them instead.
- It adds a simple subdivision, a UV Project modifier with the photo's aspect, and an unlit material that is transparent outside the frame.
- A second, movable render camera is created. Move it to get parallax while the photo stays where it belongs.

**3. Remap**

- Remap bakes the projection into a regular UV map and texture, then removes the UV Project modifier.
- The object no longer depends on the projection camera: move it, bend it or paint on it.
- Surfaces the projection camera never saw (back faces, faces hidden behind other objects) get a flat Hidden colour instead of stretched photo; plug your own colour or texture into the Hidden node.
- Textures are saved next to your .blend file, or packed into it if the file has never been saved.

**Also included**

- Swap X/Y, camera height, pick the world origin on the photo, scale from a known distance.
- Focal length from a known size (two ground segments), from EXIF (35 mm equivalent) or entered by hand.
- Lens shift: renders with parallel verticals stay level, and an optional third line pair solves the shift of cropped or shifted photos.
- Each step is a single undo step, and the calibration is saved with the .blend file.
- Works offline: OpenCV is bundled, so nothing has to be installed.

**Limitations**

- Lens distortion is not corrected. With converging verticals and only two line pairs, the optical centre is assumed at the centre of the image.
- Areas the projection camera could not see show a flat colour after Remap, not real content; paint them or plug in a texture.
- Before Remap, the live projection still puts the photo on faces the projection camera cannot see.
- Objects remapped with 1.2.0 or earlier keep their old material until remapped again; in EEVEE their far faces can show through.

Source code, tests and documentation: https://github.com/ozymandi/UV-PerspectiveProjector

## Version notes (1.2.1)

- Fix: in EEVEE, a projected or remapped object no longer shows its far faces through its near faces (for example the front of a box through its back when seen from behind). Objects remapped with 1.2.0 or earlier keep their old material until remapped again.

## Version notes (1.2.0)

- Visibility mask in Remap: back faces and surfaces hidden behind other objects no longer get stretched or wrong photo. They show a Hidden colour (by default the median of the visible part) that you can replace with any colour or texture.
- Grazing threshold (degrees, off by default): also treat surfaces seen almost edge-on as hidden, for cleaner fly-throughs over long floors and walls.
- Your own lights do not affect the mask; the view through the projection camera does not change.

## Version notes (1.1.0)

- Lens shift: when vertical lines are parallel (typical for architectural renders), the camera stays level and the horizon offset becomes vertical shift instead of a tilt.
- Optional third pair of vanishing lines: with three converging directions the optical centre is solved, so shifted and cropped photos calibrate correctly.
- Focal length from a known size: mark two ground segments with their real lengths to solve one-point (corridor, street) photos without EXIF.
- The panel shows where the focal length and the lens shift came from.
- Fixes: a first calibration that cannot be solved still creates the camera, so the line editor shows the photo; Remap no longer gives the minimum texture size to floors and walls that start behind the projection camera.

## Version notes (1.0.0)

First release.

- Automatic vanishing-point calibration with a manual line editor as fallback.
- Projection onto a frame-fitted card or selected meshes, with a separate movable render camera.
- Remap: bake the projection into regular UVs.
- Blender 4.2 LTS to 5.x on Windows, macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel) and Linux.
